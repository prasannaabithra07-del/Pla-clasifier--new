The problem: inboxes are full and filters are black boxes

Most spam filters are trained models. They work, but when one blocks a real email or lets a scam through, nobody can point to the reason.

Phishing

Fake urgent mails that ask for a password, an OTP or card details. One click can cost real money.

Spam

Promotional mail from unknown senders. Not dangerous, but it buries the mail you need to read.

No reason given

A learned model cannot say which rule fired. Hardware-style logic can: each output comes from named product terms.

Our solution: rules burnt into two planes

Each email becomes six bits. The AND plane checks patterns (product terms). The OR plane joins terms into three outputs. A priority encoder picks one label, and anything that matches no term goes to a person.

    How it works

    1. 1

      Prepare binary features

      Every email is reduced to six yes/no clues (link, urgent wording, unknown sender, attachment, credential request, promotional wording). The dataset is synthetic and generated with a fixed seed (2201), so anyone can reproduce it.

    2. 2

      Define the rules

      Each rule is a product term such as A·B·E. A literal can be true, complemented, or a don't-care, which is how one row covers many input combinations.

    3. 3

      Map product terms to outputs

      The OR plane connects terms to Phishing, Spam or Safe. One term can feed one output; several terms can feed the same output.

    4. 4

      Classify the records

      The priority encoder turns the three output lines into one label. When no term fires, the record is flagged for review instead of being guessed.

    5. 5

      Evaluate accuracy and coverage

      The results dashboard compares predictions with the true labels and shows a confusion matrix, per-class quality and how often each term fires.
